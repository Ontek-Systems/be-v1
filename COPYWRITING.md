# Blissful Escapes copy brief

Every word on the site follows this. Read it, write the copy like a good travel writer would,
check it against the bans, done. This is a brief, not a checklist: if a sentence reads well and
breaks no hard ban, it is right.

## Who is speaking

Emma Carrigan, based in Ormskirk, Lancashire, with Sylvia alongside her. Two named people planning
expensive trips that matter to the people taking them. The site should sound like a professional
firm run by warm people: the warmth comes from specifics (their names, Ormskirk, seven days a
week), not from chatty phrasing.

The benchmark line, which Emma loved:

> Boutique travel planning, personally handled. Based in Ormskirk, Lancashire, available seven
> days a week.

Plain, specific, assured, and warm because the facts are warm. Everything should sound like that.

## How to write it

- **Specific beats general.** Real places, real numbers, real names. "Game drives in the Sabi
  Sands" not "unforgettable wildlife experiences".
- **Let it flow.** Mix sentence lengths and join related thoughts with and, so, because, which.
  Stacks of short sentences sound machine written.
- **Short headings.** Section labels are one or two nouns ("Destinations", "About us"). Headings
  stay under about eight words, don't explain what the reader can already see, and take no
  full stop.
- **Say what we do, not what we aren't.** Don't answer complaints nobody raised (call centres,
  hold music, scripts).
- **"We" for the business, "you" for the reader.** "I" only in Emma's own bio or quotes.
- **Destination and holiday type copy can be richer**: second person, present tense, the day in
  the order you'd live it. Intros around 90 to 140 words, sub sections 50 to 90.

The model for descriptive copy is the paid copy from Emma's old site:

> Spectacular train journeys take visitors to remote hill stations, where life runs at a slow
> pace in the rolling hills. Leopards, sloth bears, elephants and sambar provide interesting game
> drives in Yala and Udawalawe. Golden beaches line the southern shores.

> The beauty of safaris is the opportunity to fall into the natural rhythm of the bush. Enjoy
> twice daily wildlife activities, then take a well deserved siesta beneath the warm African sun.

Lift and retune the old long haul pages (Thailand, Sri Lanka, the safari pages) and "Luxury is a
feeling" from the concierge page. The old European country pages, the old "Why Us?" page and
anything with exclamation marks or typos was Emma's own draft writing and is not reused.

## Hard bans

1. No em dashes, en dashes, or hyphens in prose ("five star", "world class"). No exclamation marks.
2. No booking language. The site takes enquiries: plan, enquire, tell us, start. Never book, buy,
   search, check availability.
3. Banned words: unforgettable, breathtaking, stunning, hidden gem, nestled, curated, seamless,
   elevate, wanderlust, journey (meaning holiday), paradise, "dream holiday awaits", "sit back and
   relax", "we've got you covered", passionate.
4. No alliterative triples, slogans, puns, or wordplay.
5. No rhetorical question headings ("Ready for a holiday?").
6. No invented facts. Only what is in the facts table. Anything TBC gets confirmed, not guessed.
7. Never describe stock photography as client photos or real trips.
8. Never imply we only book the listed destinations. They book anywhere.
9. No Hays Travel, Holidaysplease, or pandemic references. No emoji.

## House style

British English. Sentence case headings. Spell out one to ten, but use numerals when the number is
the proof (29 five star reviews, over 600 suppliers) and always for money (£36,000), dates and
phone numbers. "Ormskirk, Lancashire" in full on first mention on a page. ABTA and ATOL in caps
with their numbers.

## Page notes

- **Hero:** one promise about the person planning, one sub line with a proof fact, one enquiry
  action.
- **Proof goes high** on the homepage: the 29 reviews and the accreditations, not buried in the
  footer.
- **Why book with us** (About page only): five numbered reasons, each a short heading plus two or
  three sentences. A real person, reachable seven days a week and on WhatsApp. A specialist
  (name the specialisms). Protected (ABTA, ATOL, Azure Indies, with numbers). Connected (over 600
  suppliers, someone to call when something goes wrong abroad). Straight about money (no hidden
  fees, no admin charges, no premium for the service).
- **About:** Emma and Sylvia's bios are supplied by Emma in first person. Frame them with real
  life, casual holiday photos, not headshots.
- **Destinations:** by region, no more than ten, framed as the places clients ask for most, with a
  line inviting anywhere else.
- **Holiday types:** each one gets a genuinely different angle, not the same paragraph with the
  noun swapped. Include solos.
- **Case studies:** who and the occasion, what they wanted, what Emma actually did (the detail is
  the product), the outcome in the client's own review words. If it could be about any client,
  it isn't finished. Heading: "What our clients say" or "29 five star reviews".
- **Contact:** email, mobile and WhatsApp at the top, Emma and Sylvia each with their own number.
- **Forms:** name and email required, phone optional, departure airport, dates with a flexible
  option, no budget field. Free text prompt: "Tell us about the trip. Who is travelling, and is it
  for a special occasion, a wedding, a honeymoon, a fiftieth?"
